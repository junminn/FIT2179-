Malaysia's Weather & Air Quality

This dashboard is designed for Malaysian residents or anyone to monitor rising temperatures and air pollutant levels. We track these changes to prevent heat-related illnesses and respiratory risks by analyzing these trends through interactive spatial and seasonal visualizations.

Sun Safety: UV Exposure Categories

Understanding the intensity of ultraviolet radiation is vital. We can observe safety guidelines and protection requirements for different index levels. This helps us to be aware when we walk outside.

💡 Key Insights

  • The Reflection Effect: UV reflects off water and pavement, potentially doubling exposure.
  • The Cloud Myth: Up to 80% of UV rays pass through light clouds.
  • The 10-4 Rule: UV intensity is highest between 10:00 AM and 4:00 PM.
  • Cumulative Damage: Protection should be a daily habit, not just for extreme days.

💡 Did you know?

  • Low: No damage is expected. You can safely stay outside.
  • Moderate - High: Some precaution needed for people with sensitive skin. Seek shade during midday hours. Put on a shirt, use sun screen and a hat.
  • Very High - Extreme: Avoid unnecessary exposure especially during midday hours. Make sure you seek shade. Shirt, sun screen and hat are a must.

Climate Profiles Across Malaysia

This ridge chart reveals the distribution of climate patterns across different regions, highlighting seasonal fluctuations in air quality. Pollutants reaching the Hazardous or Very Unhealthy levels (red/purple segments) trigger mandatory escalation to the Department of Environment (DOE).

During these peaks, PM2.5 particles are so small they bypass the lungs and enter the bloodstream directly. Reporting isn't just for data; it triggers legal Cloud Seeding operations and school closure protocols across affected Malaysian states. Segments in the Good and Moderate ranges represent concentrations generally safe for the public.

While labeled safe, Moderate levels can still cause "silent inflammation" in sensitive groups. Malaysia’s MAQI standards are specifically calibrated for tropical humidity, which can trap pollutants closer to the ground, making "Moderate" air feel heavier than in dryer climates.
