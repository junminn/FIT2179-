Malaysia's Weather & Air Quality

This dashboard is designed for Malaysian residents or anyone to monitor rising temperatures and air pollutant levels. We track these changes to prevent heat-related illnesses and respiratory risks by analyzing these trends through interactive spatial and seasonal visualizations.

Sun Safety: UV Exposure Categories

Understanding the intensity of ultraviolet radiation is vital.

We can observe safety guidelines and protection requirements for different index levels.

💡 Key Insights

  • The Reflection Effect: UV reflects off water and pavement, potentially doubling exposure.
  • The Cloud Myth: Up to 80% of UV rays pass through light clouds.
  • The 10-4 Rule: UV intensity is highest between 10:00 AM and 4:00 PM.
  • Cumulative Damage:Protection should be a daily habit, not just for extreme days.

💡 Did you know?

  • Low: No damage is expected. You can safely stay outside.
  • Moderate - High:Some precaution needed for people with sensitive skin. Seek shade during midday hours. Put on a shirt, use sun screen and a hat.
  • Very High - Extreme: Avoid unnecessary exposure especially during midday hours. Make sure you seek shade. Shirt, sun screen and hat are a must.

Climate Profiles Across Malaysia

This ridge chart reveals the distribution of climate patterns across different regions, highlighting seasonal fluctuations in air quality.

💡 Key Insights

  • Mandatory Escalation: Pollutants reaching Hazardous or Very Unhealthy levels trigger immediate alarms to the DOE.
  • Crisis Protocols: These strict data reporting peaks trigger critical cloud seeding operations and school closures.
  • Safe Baselines: Segments in the Good and Moderate ranges represent concentrations generally safe for the public.

💡 Did you know?

  • PM2.5 Dangers: During spikes, particles are so tiny they bypass lung filters and enter your bloodstream directly.
  • Silent Inflammation: Labeled safe, Moderate levels can still cause underlying respiratory irritation in highly sensitive groups.
  • Tropical Humidity: Malaysia’s MAQI standards account for thick moisture, which traps heavy pollutants closer to ground level.
